# AI Infrastructure Energy
**Date:** 2026-06-07

## Key Examples
A widely shared United Nations University report projects that global data centers consumed approximately 448 TWh of electricity in 2025—more than Saudi Arabia’s entire annual usage—with AI accounting for roughly one-fifth of that demand. By 2030 the forecast rises to 945 TWh, comparable to Japan’s current total electricity consumption, with AI responsible for about 40 percent. Water usage for cooling is expected to roughly double to 2.5 trillion gallons per year while the land footprint of data centers could expand from 6,900 km² to over 14,500 km². The report highlights that constant high-power GPU and accelerator operation drives both energy and water demand, warning that rapid AI deployment could strain resources especially in already-stressed regions despite potential efficiency gains elsewhere.

Hyperscalers issued more than $155 billion in unsecured bonds in May alone, representing a greater than 45 percent increase over 2025 totals, with some AI infrastructure bond sales becoming four times oversubscribed. Data center operator TeraWulf successfully sold junk bonds and is now pursuing leveraged loans to fund further expansion. Intel and Hon Hai (Foxconn) announced a broad collaboration spanning silicon, rack-scale systems, Edge AI, and Physical AI platforms. Investor attention is also turning to smaller names such as $GRRR and optical networking players like Astera Labs as the capital influx continues.

## Insights
The scale of the projected resource demands—nearly doubling electricity consumption while more than doubling water use—reveals that AI infrastructure growth is no longer a marginal add-on but a primary driver reshaping national energy and water planning. Financing patterns show that traditional bond markets are now treating AI data centers as core infrastructure assets on par with utilities, yet the environmental externalities remain largely unpriced in current investment models.

## Notable Opinions
- Political scrutiny is emerging around taxpayer-backed AI data centers, with Canadian MP Dr. Leslyn Lewis demanding details on energy demand, privacy, jobs, and technological sovereignty implications.
- Speculative threads explore placing data centers in space to bypass terrestrial power and water constraints, noting that heat dissipation without an atmosphere remains the central engineering challenge.
