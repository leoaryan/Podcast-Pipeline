# AI Infrastructure Energy
**Date:** 2026-06-26

## Key Examples
Samsung announced plans to invest $648 billion in South Korea over the next decade, with a heavy focus on AI infrastructure, semiconductors, and chips as a national strategic bet to dominate future AI capabilities. This scale of commitment underscores how governments and corporations are treating AI hardware and energy supply chains as geopolitical priorities rather than purely commercial plays. The investment spans memory, logic, and related ecosystem elements critical for scaling data centers. https://x.com/IlyaShvarev/status/2070401934540804420

Discussions highlight that US data center electricity use is projected to rise from roughly 4% of total power in 2023 toward 7–12% by 2028, equivalent to the load of tens of millions of additional homes, with grids in key regions already under strain. Globally, data centers currently account for about 2% of electricity, of which 15–20% is attributed to AI workloads, with IEA-linked projections showing demand nearly doubling from ~415 TWh in 2024 to ~945 TWh by 2030. These figures drive conversations about new legislation requiring AI data centers to internalize more grid costs and interest in alternative energy solutions like fuel cells and battery storage. https://x.com/grok/status/2070327271517241793 https://x.com/WoodnuttStacey/status/2069956513448460410

Environmental impacts are quantified in posts noting massive water use for cooling (1–5 million gallons per day per large facility), land footprints potentially exceeding 14,500 km², and up to 2.5 million tonnes of e-waste annually, with water consumption potentially rivaling the basic domestic needs of 1.3 billion people by the end of the decade. These specifics frame AI infrastructure not just as a technical scaling challenge but as a resource competition issue with other sectors. https://x.com/WoodnuttStacey/status/2069956513448460410

## Insights
Power infrastructure has emerged as the binding constraint ahead of chip supply, shifting investment and policy focus from semiconductor fabrication to energy generation, transmission, and alternative sources; this reveals that the AI scaling curve is now limited more by electrons than transistors, forcing hyperscalers and nations to treat power procurement as a core competency.

The overlap with crypto/Web3 projects like RaxFinance, which tokenizes GPU capacity, data center power, and hydroelectric assets into on-chain yield-bearing RWAs, illustrates an emerging financialization layer that could accelerate capital formation for AI energy assets while introducing new volatility and regulatory questions around infrastructure ownership.

## Notable Opinions
Critics point out that hyperscalers are constructing some of the largest resource-intensive projects while facing less climate scrutiny than other industries, highlighting a potential inconsistency in sustainability narratives around AI growth. https://x.com/WoodnuttStacey/status/2069956513448460410
