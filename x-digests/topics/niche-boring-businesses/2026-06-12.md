# Niche & Boring Businesses
**Date:** 2026-06-12

## Key Examples
Apollo valued Apex Service Partners (HVAC, plumbing, electrical services across 46 states with ~$3B revenue) at approximately $10 billion. Original investors achieved 23x returns on what @Blackintus described as “the most boring business in America.” The moat stems from essential services where price becomes irrelevant when AC breaks in 95°F heat or a drain clogs. The post points to a ~$700B U.S. home services market and public comps like Watsco ($WSO), MSC Industrial ($MSM), and W.W. Grainger ($GWW), showing how private equity roll-ups in mundane sectors can generate outsized returns because demand is stable, recession-resistant, and nearly impossible to disrupt with AI or software.

@TheIcahnist highlighted Harold Simmons, who turned $5K into $10B through forensic accounting focused on “boring small caps.” The approach involves deep scrutiny of overlooked companies in unglamorous industries where information asymmetry is high and competition from sophisticated investors is low. This contrasts with high-profile tech or growth investing where attention and capital are concentrated.

## Insights
The appeal of boring businesses lies in their resistance to technological disruption and their inelastic demand during economic downturns, creating durable cash flows that justify premium valuations in private equity despite low glamour. The 23x return on Apex demonstrates that execution in fragmented essential services can compound more reliably than many software or consumer internet bets.

Forensic accounting on small caps in mundane sectors remains a repeatable edge because these companies receive less analyst coverage and media attention, allowing patient capital to identify mispricings that flashier sectors have arbitraged away.
