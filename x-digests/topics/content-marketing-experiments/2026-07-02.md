# Content Marketing Experiments
**Date:** 2026-07-02

## Key Examples
A Substack employee in a data/product role (@philipnguyen) ran a small 3% experiment removing his content from the notes feed, resulting in increased time spent and daily active users. This counterintuitive outcome suggests the platform’s discovery algorithms are sensitive to content quality or type, where adding more content is not always better. The post is rare in publicly sharing internal-style test results. @philipnguyen shared the exact experiment parameters and metrics.

An email holdout experiment resurfaced from 2014 where a growth team split their list: one side received normal marketing emails while the other received none for 60 days. The no-email group still purchased at ~85% the rate of the email group, leading the team to conclude that most “attributed” email revenue would have happened anyway. They shifted focus from volume to quality and stopped over-investing in sends. @0Venkata posted the details, emphasizing that few teams run this test because results can be politically difficult.

A detailed LinkedIn content system generated 672 B2B SaaS demo calls in 30 days using two founder accounts posting daily (~60 posts/month) with content structured by awareness stage (problem-unaware → pain-aware → solution-aware/proof). Multiple lead magnets were tested weekly with strong hooks and video proof, followed by a human-first DM sequence that framed calls as “setup calls” instead of sales calls, lifting show-up rates from the 50s to the 80s. @paolo_scales shared the full interconnected system playbook.

One email marketer doubled newsletter conversion rate from 4.4% to 8.1% in under two months through systematic learning from past sends. Another Reg A+ email funnel achieved a 520% conversion lift (0.8% → 4.96%) via behavioral segmentation, obsessive subject-line/CTA testing, SMS follow-ups, and webinar sequences. @Copy_Stephen and @GT_Crowdfunding posted the before-and-after metrics and testing methodology.

## Insights
The incrementality test results highlight that many content marketing attribution models overstate lift because they ignore baseline behavior, leading sophisticated operators to prioritize quality and testing infrastructure over raw send volume or posting frequency.

Treating content-to-lead as a full interconnected system (awareness-stage content + tested magnets + human DM framing + setup-call positioning) rather than isolated tactics explains why single improvements often fail while the combined approach delivers outsized results like 672 calls in 30 days.
