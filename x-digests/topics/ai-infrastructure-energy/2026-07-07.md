# AI Infrastructure Energy
**Date:** 2026-07-07

## Key Examples
The IEA projects global data center electricity consumption will reach 945 TWh by 2030 (nearly triple the combined annual use of Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Nigeria), driven by AI workloads, with additional massive water footprints for cooling (~9.3 trillion liters/year). Posts highlight that grid and power infrastructure are now the primary bottleneck ahead of GPUs, with hyperscaler CAPEX surging past $1.1 trillion by 2027 according to Morgan Stanley; utilities are struggling to keep pace, leading data centers to pursue dedicated or off-grid solutions. https://x.com/TIDC2026/status/2074359001417134564

Microsoft announced a 2.7 GW data center project in West Texas supported by a 20-year natural gas supply deal with Chevron, while also restarting Three Mile Island; separately, Meta has agreements for up to 6.6 GW of nuclear capacity and Anthropic signed a major long-term deal with TeraWulf for nuclear-powered capacity. These deals illustrate the scale of demand and the shift toward nuclear as the densest, always-on, low-carbon option for AI workloads. https://x.com/world_poli_hub/status/2074384731827814611

Nvidia and Valar Atomics are exploring a 30 MW nuclear-powered AI data center in Utah using the Ward250 small modular reactor design, emphasizing minimal water consumption. Broader discussion positions advanced SMRs as the solution for reliable power, with pipelines growing from 25 GW in 2024 toward 45 GW targeted by 2026. https://x.com/weareracetoagi/status/2074387967217398050

## Insights
Power generation and delivery have overtaken chip supply as the constraining factor for AI scaling; efficiency gains at the model or chip level are being outpaced by the sheer volume of agentic workloads, which can consume up to 136.5× more energy per query than standard chat interactions due to multiple LLM calls, tool uses, and iterations.

Nuclear revival (including SMRs and restarts of existing plants) is no longer hypothetical but is actively being contracted by hyperscalers, creating a new investment and infrastructure theme centered on energy density, grid independence, and water efficiency rather than pure compute metrics.
