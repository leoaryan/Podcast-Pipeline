# AI Infrastructure Energy
**Date:** 2026-06-28

## Key Examples
Tech job openings rose 14% overall in 2026 while hardware engineering roles surged 52% due to AI infrastructure buildout, with memory and semiconductor companies like Samsung and Micron crossing $1 trillion market caps as high-bandwidth memory demand for GPUs transforms former commodity businesses. Compute scarcity is emerging as a competitive moat, with Google reportedly limiting Meta’s access to Gemini models due to resource constraints. A single large AI-focused data center can consume as much power as 100,000 homes, with U.S. data centers already at ~4.4% of national electricity in 2023 and projections reaching 6.7–12%+ by 2028–2030; global data center electricity use is forecast to roughly double to ~945 TWh by 2030. A 1 GW-scale GB200-class AI facility could cost ~$38B+ (including $12B land/facility/power infrastructure, $21B servers, $5B networking) with power per GPU around 1.6 kW effective in dense deployments. Water use for cooling at mid-to-large facilities reaches hundreds of thousands to millions of gallons per day, often in water-stressed regions, while backup diesel generators add NOx, particulates, noise (55–90+ dB), light pollution, and e-waste. https://x.com/DB_Wales/status/2071126758896607509 https://x.com/investoscopeX/status/2071124689079107696 https://x.com/WhalorAlert/status/2071126400761844181 https://x.com/MatthewGoedtel/status/2070479383739933176 https://x.com/j0ker937/status/2070637554668323195 https://x.com/AI_Investor_/status/2070375283107004611

## Insights
The localized physical costs (electricity, water, pollution, land use) of AI infrastructure stand in contrast to the global and digital nature of its benefits, creating uneven community impacts that are intensifying scrutiny even as energy-sector investments surge with some related stocks doubling on sustained demand expectations. Compute access itself functions as strategic power, turning infrastructure scarcity into a binding constraint that influences model availability and competitive positioning beyond pure algorithmic advances.

## Notable Opinions
Compute remains power, and the scarcest resource in AI.
