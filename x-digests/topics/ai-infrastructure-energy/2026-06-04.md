# AI Infrastructure Energy
**Date:** 2026-06-04

## Key Examples
Global data center electricity consumption stood at approximately 415 TWh in 2024, representing 1.5% of worldwide electricity, with projections showing a rise to roughly 945 TWh by 2030 under IEA base case assumptions. AI accounts for up to 40% of this growth in some estimates, driven primarily by the shift from traditional CPUs at 150-200W to AI GPUs at 700W for H100-class chips and up to 1,200W for next-generation Blackwell variants. This 6-8x increase per chip forces rack power densities from a few kW to 40-100+ kW, necessitating liquid cooling and creating localized heat island effects where up to 40% of facility energy goes to cooling systems.

Google reported a 27% jump in data center electricity consumption in 2024 directly attributable to AI workloads, resulting in emissions 51% above 2019 levels. Microsoft responded by signing a 140 MW U.S. solar power purchase agreement specifically to meet AI compute demand, while Adani in India is pursuing a vertically integrated strategy combining renewables, transmission, and a planned 2 GW data center platform. These examples illustrate how hyperscalers are moving from grid reliance to "bring your own power" strategies including dedicated microgrids and nuclear co-location.

A single large AI data center can consume millions of gallons of water daily for cooling, with UK projections estimating data centers could reach 9.3 trillion litres annually alongside 399 million tons of associated CO₂ emissions. Grid interconnection queues for new facilities now average 4-10 years versus 2-3 year build times, with high-voltage equipment lead times stretching to five years, making inference workloads the dominant long-term load rather than one-time training runs.

## Insights
The marginal megawatt is now more intelligence, meaning AI provides a clear, scalable use case for additional electricity generation after decades of relatively flat demand in many regions, fundamentally changing the economics of power infrastructure investment.

On-device and edge inference delivers 100-1,000× better energy efficiency per query compared to cloud round-trips, positioning sovereign and local-first approaches as essential complements to centralized frontier training rather than replacements.

## Notable Opinions
"Grid interconnection for new data centers averages 4–10 years (vs 2–3 year build times)" highlights the infrastructure bottleneck that is now the primary constraint on AI scaling, pushing operators toward co-located generation solutions.
