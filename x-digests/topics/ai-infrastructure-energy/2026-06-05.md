# AI Infrastructure Energy
**Date:** 2026-06-05

## Key Examples
Global data center electricity use stood at roughly 415 TWh in 2024 and is projected to reach ~945 TWh by 2030, driven primarily by AI workloads. Google reported a 27% jump in data center electricity consumption in 2024 alone, pushing emissions above 2019 levels. Modern AI GPU racks now demand 40–100+ kW compared to traditional CPU racks, with next-gen chips reaching 700–1,200W per GPU. Grid interconnection queues average 4–10 years while transformers face multi-year lead times, turning power availability into the primary bottleneck over chip supply. https://x.com/ResGeoPol/status/2062181177855762763

Microsoft deployed closed-loop cooling systems in new AI data centers that consume as little water annually as one restaurant. China placed sealed subsea data centers near Shanghai for passive ocean cooling, cutting cooling energy by 30–90% while pairing with offshore wind. NVIDIA released DSX OS focused on tokens per watt efficiency, multi-tenant reliability, and faster deployment. Photonic and valleytronic architectures are discussed as potential 90% energy reductions by replacing electricity with light for computation. https://x.com/tomshardware/status/2062483375013957674

Hyperscalers increasingly bring their own power through dedicated microgrids, direct nuclear or natural gas deals, and on-site generation. Optical interconnects are emerging as the next critical layer after compute and memory innovations, with Marvell partnering with NVIDIA on solutions as copper hits bandwidth limits. Local opposition is rising, with one California city permanently banning new data centers. https://x.com/MarvellTech/status/2062050537617625440

## Insights
The conversation has shifted from a GPU and model race to one where power density, grid access, water rights, and permitting now determine competitive advantage more than raw compute capacity, creating a new class of infrastructure plays around behind-the-meter generation and advanced cooling.

Efficiency gains from software like concise prompts and better orchestration are seen as essential mitigations rather than afterthoughts, because inference loads are projected to dominate long-term energy demand over training.

## Notable Opinions
Multiple analysts argue that reliable low-carbon baseload power, especially nuclear, is non-negotiable for sustainable AI scaling, with sector rotation favoring optical and infrastructure suppliers over pure GPU names on certain days.
