# Content Marketing Experiments
**Date:** 2026-06-22

## Key Examples
@linafahizul documented a compounding A/B testing approach where secondary signals from one test directly inform the next hypothesis. In the first test, removing an extra collection page and sending traffic straight to the product page delivered +25.54% conversion rate, though average order value dipped slightly. The team then used the insight that shorter journeys performed better and tested sending traffic to a bundles page instead. This produced +30.80% conversion rate while also increasing AOV. The methodology treats every test not as an isolated win/loss but as data generation for the next iteration, allowing brands to achieve sustained 2-3x lifts through sequential refinement rather than hoping for single big wins.

@Hassan_Desgro ran a landing page redesign experiment for a medspa using Framer specifically built for Meta ad traffic. The previous generic page was replaced with one featuring dedicated conversion elements and intent-matched messaging. Over 30 days the new page generated 85 leads at 14.8% conversion rate — an 87% improvement. The before/after comparison isolates the impact of purpose-built pages versus repurposed general site sections.

@mbaasapfinance shared a founder’s headline test that shortened the homepage headline from 14 words to 6 words with no other product changes. Conversion rate rose from 1.2% to 3.8% within two weeks. The experiment demonstrates that clarity and reduced cognitive load can produce outsized lifts when the rest of the funnel remains constant.

## Insights
The highest-performing experiments treat testing as a hypothesis refinement engine rather than a series of independent trials. By mining secondary metrics (such as AOV movement alongside CVR) to generate the next test, teams create compounding gains that isolated tests cannot achieve. This explains why some brands see dramatic sustained improvements while others plateau despite running many tests.

## Notable Opinions
"Bringing in product, sales, and support teams (who know real customer wants, objections, and questions) can boost engagement 40–60%. Silos kill results." — @Sixtylicious on why content created in isolation underperforms.
