# AI Design Creativity
**Date:** 2026-06-30

## Key Examples
@Crownzdesigns shared a Day 1 video demo of testing Framer’s new AI agent in an “AI-first workflow” for building UI/UX and product interfaces. The demonstration documented real-time iteration using the agent for layout, component generation, and prototyping, receiving 34 likes and active replies asking for workflow specifics and limitations observed. https://x.com/i/status/2071739611781443810

@AIandDesign launched ios.ai-created.com, a portfolio site showcasing eight iOS apps built end-to-end with AI tools. The site serves as a concrete case study of using generative AI for app design, iconography, and user flows, highlighting what current tools can and cannot achieve without heavy human direction. https://x.com/i/status/2071745340727005495

Professional designer @Staremmy12 posted a video critique of “messy AI design” in social media graphics and promotional flyers, arguing that low-quality AI outputs create immediate distrust and reduce prospect engagement with brands. He contrasts this with human-designed work that maintains consistency and perceived professionalism, positioning AI as better suited for internal ideation than client-facing deliverables. https://x.com/i/status/2071728095317369218

## Insights
The split between enthusiastic early adopters experimenting with no-code AI agents like Framer and skeptical professionals concerned about brand perception reveals a deeper tension in AI-assisted design: speed and iteration versus trust and polish. Projects that succeed appear to treat AI as a rapid prototyping layer rather than the final output, with human oversight applied at the brand and quality gates.

Portfolio experiments like ios.ai-created.com provide measurable artifacts for evaluating current model capabilities in constrained domains such as iOS app design, where consistent visual language and platform conventions are easier to enforce than in open branding work.

## Notable Opinions
@Staremmy12 emphasized that first-impression distrust from AI-generated visuals actively harms client acquisition, suggesting the current generation of tools still requires significant human refinement before replacing traditional design services in client work.
