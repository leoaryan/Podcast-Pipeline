# AI Infrastructure Energy
**Date:** 2026-07-01

## Key Examples
Goldman Sachs estimates approximately $7.6 trillion will be invested globally in AI infrastructure (compute, data centers, and power) between 2026 and 2031. Users positioned decentralized networks such as Bittensor as the “next layer” beyond traditional centralized capex. https://x.com/TaoOutsider/status/2072201730591211688

Brookfield and Bloom Energy expanded their AI infrastructure partnership to a $25 billion commitment (a 5x increase from prior levels), focusing on fuel-cell and clean power solutions specifically for data centers. The scale of the expansion signals accelerating demand for reliable on-site or behind-the-meter generation. https://x.com/wey_how12640/status/2072138388271251918

@scalar_field_ mapped the supply-chain migration downstream from GPUs into HBM, power delivery, cooling, packaging, probe cards, substrates, MLCCs, OSATs, and grid infrastructure. Humanoid robots are expected to layer additional demand for batteries, sensors, and power electronics, meaning bottlenecks are simply relocating rather than disappearing. https://x.com/scalar_field_/status/2072179601007325565

## Insights
The conversation has matured beyond GPU counts to full-stack infrastructure constraints, particularly power generation, transmission, and specialized components. Capital is now chasing not only chips but also the physical and electrical substrate required to run them at scale, creating investment opportunities in areas that were previously secondary.

Decentralized and sovereign compute models (e.g., “Bring Your Own Mac” Power Nodes using existing M4 Pro hardware) emerge as responses to both energy constraints and regulatory/compliance pressures. These approaches leverage installed consumer hardware for private, localized inference, reducing reliance on hyperscale data centers and their associated grid demands.
