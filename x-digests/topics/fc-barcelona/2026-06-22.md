# FC Barcelona
**Date:** 2026-06-22

## Key Examples
Hansi Flick’s tactical system at Barcelona emphasizes high-intensity pressing combined with a high defensive line and offside trap. In build-up, Frenkie de Jong frequently drops into a half-space or left center-back role within a 5-at-the-back shape (or as a 6 in a 4) to organize play and break opponent presses. Pedri pairs with him to control tempo and provide moments of temporization. Dani Olmo receives the ball higher, often in front of the box, drawing from Spain’s setup under Luis de la Fuente which borrows short passing sequences and third-man runs from Flick’s ideas. Lamine Yamal is given freedom to drift into CAM areas without rigid positional instructions, echoing Messi’s historical movement.

The club is undergoing a "revolution" in physical preparation under Flick. Julio Tous, Germán Fernández, and Rafa Maldonado are departing while Pepe Conde remains. This change directly addresses sustaining the high-intensity style across a long season, particularly in Champions League matches where the high line and pressing can be exploited in transitions. Analysts note that Barcelona sometimes lacks the physical robustness Bayern possessed under Flick, making timing and risk management critical.

## Insights
Flick’s approach blends positional play elements with more direct, high-pressing structures, creating hybrid roles for midfielders like de Jong that serve both defensive organization and build-up. The physical preparation overhaul is not merely support staff turnover but a deliberate response to the sustainability demands of the system, revealing that tactical identity and athletic conditioning are tightly coupled in modern high-pressing setups.

## Notable Opinions
Flick is viewed as less of a pure risk-taker compared to Pep, Enrique, or Bielsa, with coaches already adopting elements such as Olmo’s positioning, Pedri dropping deep, and false 9 usage in their own systems.
