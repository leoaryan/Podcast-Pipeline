# AI Infrastructure Energy
**Date:** 2026-06-23

## Key Examples
Analysis from @thehedgeberg and others notes that US data center electricity demand is projected to rise from 167 TWh in 2023 to 376 TWh by 2030, with power supply (not chips) emerging as the primary scaling constraint because batteries only shift energy temporally without generating new supply. NVIDIA’s upcoming Vera Rubin AI data centers are described as extremely capital- and energy-intensive, with a single gigawatt-scale installation potentially costing $47–50 billion to build and carrying annual electricity bills approaching $1.3 billion, where hardware depreciation reportedly runs 6× higher than power costs. https://x.com/thehedgeberg/status/2068731470533808388 https://x.com/wccftech/status/2068502149290700928

IEA base-case scenarios project total data center consumption reaching ~945 TWh by 2030, with AI/accelerated computing driving most growth at ~30% annual rates; AI-focused data centers alone consumed roughly 155 TWh in 2025 (~0.5% of global electricity). A UN report circulating on X projects data center power consumption doubling by 2030 (roughly Japan’s current entire electricity usage), with AI accounting for about 40% of that load and water consumption reaching 9.3 trillion liters annually. https://x.com/grok/status/2069121877709136243 https://x.com/AIxWellbeing/status/2069086754334802130

@GSusie63 summarized real vs. hyped impacts: US data centers used ~4.4% of electricity in 2023, with individual large AI hyperscale facilities consuming as much as 50k–100k+ households; demand could double or triple by 2028–2030, driving new generation while causing local grid strain. Mid-to-large data centers can use hundreds of thousands to millions of gallons per day for evaporative cooling. https://x.com/GSusie63/status/2068786629586612490

## Insights
The binding constraint on AI scaling through 2030 is shifting from model capability or chip availability to physical power generation, transmission, and siting, with second-order effects including water stress in arid regions and the need for new permitting and recycling mandates. Organizational readiness lags technical progress: a Capgemini thread notes 82% of organizations have immature AI infrastructure and only 16% have a roadmap, with trust in fully autonomous agents dropping from 43% to 27% in a year, favoring human-in-the-loop designs.

## Notable Opinions
Power infrastructure represents one of the most binding constraints on AI scaling, with discussions acknowledging both the scale of demand and the enabling benefits of AI-driven efficiency gains when siting and operations improve.
