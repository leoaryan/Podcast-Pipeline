# Content Marketing Experiments
**Date:** 2026-06-03

## Key Examples
- **"Discovered > Pitched" shift** — High-performing marketers at $1M+/yr run rate are abandoning hook → value → offer. Instead they create distribution-first content where the product appears naturally "in frame" (like an iPhone in a movie). Tactics: film "how I shipped 30 posts last week" style content where your tool is the one being used, build series around processes and results, remove explicit CTAs from high-reach content. Audiences now detect ads instantly and compare products with one AI prompt. Source: x.com/i/status/2061626830197842354
- **4-channel strategic distribution framework** — Content promotion succeeds by placing material where people are already seeking answers: (1) Search optimization targeting featured snippets, AI overviews, comparison queries; (2) Communities — genuine participation in Reddit, Slack, Discord before linking; (3) Strategic partnerships — co-create with complementary brands, provide ready-to-use assets; (4) Direct outreach — personalized, contextual, no pressure. Follows a timeline: pre-launch prep → launch day SEO/email → weeks 1-4 community/outreach → ongoing repurposing. Source: x.com/i/status/2061817839686660292
- **Content moats over content volume from @home_work_biz** — Fastest-growing companies build AI-powered ecosystems combining structured SEO + GEO content, email automation, and authority-driven systems. Visibility is fragmented across AI answers, social algorithms, and trusted creators. The "publish randomly and hope" model is dead. Winners focus on strong positioning + topic depth + distribution loops + repeated visibility. Source: x.com/home_work_biz/status/2061538319150801136

## Insights
- AI hasn't killed content marketing — it's killed *lazy* content marketing. When anyone can generate 50 blog posts in an hour, the only differentiator is distribution strategy and genuine expertise. The moat isn't volume, it's trust and signal quality.
- The 4-channel framework is notable because it treats promotion as a *product*, not an afterthought. Pre-launch partner prep, ready-to-use assets for collaborators, and channel-specific measurement make distribution a system rather than a panic.
- Content moats compound: strong positioning attracts the right audience, which generates better feedback, which creates better content. SaaS companies building depth around specific topics are seeing compounding organic growth while generalists stall.

## Notable Opinions
- "Put the product in frame the whole time — not selling, just there. Like an iPhone in a movie." — on the discovered-not-pitched approach.
- "Content promotion works when you place material where people are already seeking answers, not when you interrupt them." — from the distribution framework author.
