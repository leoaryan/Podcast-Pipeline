# AI Infrastructure Energy
**Date:** 2026-06-14

## Key Examples
Posts from @SemiconductorsX and @permanentbid highlighted that electricity demand has overtaken chip supply as the primary scaling constraint for AI. Data centers that consumed ~4% of U.S. power in 2023 are projected to reach ~17% by 2030. One megawatt of AI data-center capacity now costs approximately $11 million to build—double the 2022 figure—with individual campuses requiring up to 1,000 MW. Microsoft is restarting the Three Mile Island nuclear reactor specifically to power AI workloads, while Anthropic reportedly pays Elon Musk $1.25 billion per month for data-center capacity.

Gartner forecasts world data-center electricity use will increase 26% in 2026 alone (447 TWh to 565 TWh), with AI-optimized servers expected to consume 31% of that total and overtake traditional servers. Grid interconnection queues, water for cooling, and permitting delays are now the binding constraints—many labs “have nowhere to plug in.”

## Insights
The conversation has shifted from model performance to physical infrastructure limits. Sustained baseload power requirements favor nuclear restarts and specialized electrical contractors over traditional GPU scaling narratives. The most underpriced assets in the AI trade are now the energy supply chain, transmission upgrades, and the regional contractors capable of delivering zero-downtime power systems at scale.

## Notable Opinions
@permanentbid noted that the physical electrical and cooling infrastructure is being built primarily by smaller, often family-owned specialty contractors rather than the hyperscalers themselves, creating a “picks and shovels” opportunity that remains under-the-radar relative to model hype.
