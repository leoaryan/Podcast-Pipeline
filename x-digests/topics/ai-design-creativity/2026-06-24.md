# AI Design & Creativity
**Date:** 2026-06-24

## Key Examples
@alexpark described the rapid shift in creative AI platforms toward agentic workflows and “vibe directing” systems, noting that users tested OpenArt’s new vibe directing feature and Reve’s agentic image workflow on the same day. These systems mirror the agent loops already common in coding tools, moving the human role from detailed prompting to high-level inputs such as point-of-view, story, taste, vision, and voice. The prediction is that low-friction multi-agent orchestration covering planning, generation, and iteration will become standard across tools like Pika and Flora, commoditizing raw generation while democratizing high-quality visual storytelling.

@jilyannori, a Korean architecture designer, explicitly connected the current wave of AI agents to architectural practice, arguing that success depends on the personal workflow and system built on top of the models rather than the models themselves. This aligns with broader design commentary that competitive advantage comes from shaping AI to one’s own taste, weird ideas, and integrated workflow instead of relying on generic prompts, framing tool mastery as thinking amplification.

@Resists_AiArt shared a detailed production-grade pipeline example including a full ComfyUI-style workflow via Pastebin, a specific checkpoint from Civitai, and a highly engineered prompt for a hybrid anime-realistic cryogenic facility scene featuring holographic materials, volumetric lighting, and micro-surface details. The post demonstrates transparent sharing of concrete architecture and design decisions that others can replicate or adapt.

## Insights
The move from single-prompt interfaces to orchestrated agent loops in creative tools mirrors the evolution seen in software engineering, where planners, builders, and judges cycle iteratively. This suggests that the next bottleneck in creative AI will not be model quality but the design of personal orchestration layers that encode individual taste and domain knowledge. Practitioners in architecture and design are already mapping these patterns onto their fields, indicating cross-domain transfer of agentic methodologies.

Concrete workflow sharing, such as the ComfyUI + Civitai checkpoint examples, accelerates adoption by lowering the barrier for others to achieve comparable output quality. This open sharing culture contrasts with earlier closed prompting communities and accelerates the commoditization of basic generation while raising the bar for differentiated, taste-driven results.

## Notable Opinions
Designers emphasize that “it’s not the tool — it’s the flow you build around it,” with @relaitool noting that competitive advantage stems from integrating AI into personal weird ideas and workflows rather than following generic best practices.
