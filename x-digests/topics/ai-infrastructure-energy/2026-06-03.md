# AI Infrastructure & Energy
**Date:** 2026-06-03

## Key Examples
- **Hyperscaler capex exceeds $675B in 2026, up >60% YoY from @nickpaulussen** — Microsoft, Google, and Amazon alone exceed $300B annually in AI-related capex. Individual data centers are crossing gigawatt-scale, with 100K+ GPU clusters becoming the norm. A single 1 GW GB200-class facility costs $35-40B in upfront CapEx, with Jensen Huang projecting this rising toward $80-100B as compute density, liquid cooling, and networking demands increase. Power delivery and servers dominate the economics — the shell and core are minor costs. Source: x.com/nickpaulussen/status/2061840571648692359
- **Power is now the binding constraint, not chips** — @SupplySideAI reports that chip supply tightness is easing with TSMC/Samsung ramps, but grid interconnection queues have become the primary bottleneck. @JNovy_ details the environmental strain: a single large AI data center can consume electricity equivalent to tens of thousands of homes and pull millions of gallons of water daily for cooling. 71% of Americans now oppose new data centers nearby. Land use, carbon emissions (especially gas-backed), and local power price inflation are creating political backlash. Source: x.com/SupplySideAI/status/2061888356376588719, x.com/JNovy_/status/2061748324508532784
- **US data center power demand surging: 35 GW (2024) → 100+ GW by decade end** — @TheEnergyAI reports EIA's latest outlook shows US electricity consumption hitting records and rising consecutively through 2027, the first such streak since 2007, driven primarily by hyperscale AI data centers. US installed capacity at ~45-55 GW vs China's ~32 GW, both scaling aggressively. $150B+ in utility transmission investments announced. Goldman Sachs projects only 50-60% of scheduled additions will come online on time due to grid delays and permitting blocks. Source: x.com/TheEnergyAI/status/2061920909250969977
- **Local-first / edge inference as escape valve** — @JNovy_ frames on-device inference as a "sustainable multiplier": 100-1,000x efficiency gains versus cloud round-trips, reduced latency, improved privacy, and decreased pressure on hyperscale grids. Hardware (NPUs), smaller efficient models, and digital sovereignty policies are accelerating this shift. Frontier training stays centralized, but everyday inference moves local. Source: x.com/JNovy_/status/2061748324508532784

## Insights
- 2026 marks the definitive shift where power infrastructure, not silicon, determines the pace of AI scaling. The hyperscalers are spending like there's no tomorrow, but the grid can't keep up — $64B+ in stalled US projects, 5+ year interconnection lead times, and growing community opposition.
- The local/edge inference narrative is becoming a structural necessity, not just a nice-to-have. When 71% of Americans oppose nearby data centers and grid queues stretch to 2030, on-device compute is the only path to scaling inference capacity without political fights.
- Nuclear is having a renaissance inside AI infrastructure discussions. Constellation Energy ($CEG) and Vistra ($VST) are treated as AI plays, not just utilities. SMRs (NuScale) are discussed as the long-term solution but can't scale fast enough for the 2025-2028 crunch.
